# LobbyTeam Comprehensive Buyer FAQ

## How To Use This FAQ

This FAQ is written for a potential buyer, evaluator, operator, or advisor trying to understand LobbyTeam before adopting it.

It is designed to answer:

- Who LobbyTeam is
- What problem LobbyTeam solves
- How LobbyTeam works
- Why LobbyTeam is different from an AI receptionist
- What the coordinated agent team means in practice
- What a buyer receives
- What the product can and cannot decide

Public website answers should be shortened from this document without changing their meaning. Any capability described as configured, supported, or available must be verified against the production product and the customer’s actual account setup.

## Positioning And Identity

### What is LobbyTeam?

LobbyTeam is a coordinated digital front-door team for a business. It helps customers, visitors, prospects, and callers reach an answer, a completed request, or the right human next step across channels such as phone, website chat, email, SMS, WhatsApp, and configured kiosk, tablet, or QR-code experiences.

### Why did you create LobbyTeam?

Businesses lose momentum at the point of first contact. Calls are missed, messages wait, appointments require phone tag, visitors arrive while staff are busy, and human handoffs lose context. LobbyTeam was created to turn first contact into visible progress and clear ownership instead of leaving the next step to chance.

### What do you mean by “the front door” of a business?

The front door is the first place a customer, prospect, visitor, patient, partner, or employee reaches the business. It may be a phone line, receptionist, website, email inbox, chat window, SMS number, WhatsApp account, kiosk, tablet, or QR code. LobbyTeam helps that arrival move toward a useful next step.

### Is LobbyTeam only for businesses with a physical lobby?

No. LobbyTeam supports physical offices and online businesses. It can help a receptionist or office manager, a solopreneur, an appointment team, a field-service dispatcher, an inbound-sales team, or an after-hours support operation. “Lobby” describes the first point of contact, not only a building.

### Is LobbyTeam only for small businesses?

No, but solopreneurs and small businesses are important use cases. A solo operator may be the owner, receptionist, scheduler, salesperson, and support team at once. A larger organization may use LobbyTeam across locations, shifts, specialist teams, and regional support paths.

### What outcome does LobbyTeam sell?

LobbyTeam helps each engaged request move toward one of four outcomes: an approved answer, a completed configured task, a route to the right owner, or visible follow-through. The central promise is: your customer gets progress, your team gets context, and someone knows what should happen next.

### What is a digital decision team for a website?

A digital decision team helps website visitors understand what a business offers, connect it to their own situation, and choose the right next step. LobbyTeam can ask clarifying questions, explain approved options, share relevant resources, start a booking or inquiry workflow, and route the visitor to a human with context when the decision needs expertise.

It does not make unrestricted decisions for the visitor or replace accountable judgment. The business defines the approved knowledge, recommendations, workflows, and human-only boundaries.

### Can LobbyTeam help a visitor decide which product or service fits?

Yes, when the business provides the approved product, service, audience, and problem guidance. LobbyTeam can learn what the visitor is trying to accomplish, ask focused follow-up questions, explain relevant paths, and offer a resource, conversation, booking, or human handoff. It should guide the decision rather than claim certainty beyond the business rules.

### Is LobbyTeam an AI receptionist?

LobbyTeam can support receptionist workflows, but “AI receptionist” is too narrow. A receptionist usually describes one conversational front-desk function. LobbyTeam is a coordinated digital team that can engage a customer while specialized agents help interpret the request, apply business rules, select a next step, and prepare a human handoff.

### Is LobbyTeam a chatbot?

No. A chatbot is usually understood as a question-and-answer interface. LobbyTeam may use chat, but its purpose is broader: understand the request, handle approved work, route the request to the right person or team, preserve context, and make the next action visible.

### Is LobbyTeam a call-answering service?

Not simply. A call-answering service may answer, take a message, and pass it along. LobbyTeam is designed to understand why the person reached out, provide approved information, complete configured work, determine urgency or ownership, and route the request with useful context when a human needs to take over.

### Is LobbyTeam a replacement for a receptionist?

No. LobbyTeam works alongside receptionists, office managers, coordinators, and support teams. It can handle defined first-contact work and reduce repetitive interruption, while human staff remain responsible for judgment, relationships, exceptions, and decisions that require accountable expertise.

### Is LobbyTeam a replacement for customer-support staff?

No. It can extend the coverage of a support team and help resolve or route defined requests. Human support staff remain important for sensitive, complex, technical, commercial, emotional, or otherwise judgment-heavy situations.

### Is LobbyTeam a security guard?

No. A physical-lobby deployment can help orient visitors, answer approved questions, notify a host, or request human assistance. It does not replace security personnel, access control, identity verification, emergency response, incident handling, or building safety procedures.

## The Coordinated Digital Team

### What does “team of agents” mean?

A visible interaction may be handled by one engaging agent, while specialized digital agents work behind the scenes to interpret the request, check approved guidance, identify urgency or fit, select the configured workflow, determine the appropriate owner, and prepare the next step or human handoff.

### Is this just one bot with a new name?

No. The differentiation is not the number of agents as a technical marketing claim. The difference is the coordinated work behind the interaction: conversation, interpretation, business-rule checking, workflow selection, routing, context preservation, and follow-through operate as one front-door experience.

### What does the engaging agent do?

The engaging agent communicates with the customer through the configured channel. It welcomes the person, understands the request, asks the questions needed to move forward, provides approved information, and either completes the configured next step or brings in the appropriate workflow and human owner.

### What do the specialist agents do?

Specialist agents may help classify the request, identify what the person needs, detect urgency, find relevant approved knowledge, determine whether the request can be handled, select a routing path, and prepare context for escalation. The exact roles depend on the workflows and capabilities enabled for the account.

### Do the agents make unrestricted decisions?

No. The business defines the knowledge, approved actions, workflows, routing rules, escalation rules, and boundaries. LobbyTeam applies those rules within the configured workflow. Humans remain accountable for sensitive, technical, commercial, security, emergency, clinical, legal, and other judgment-heavy decisions.

### Can I inspect or correct what LobbyTeam decided?

The product should make the interaction, classification, routing reason, and next action inspectable to the account team wherever those capabilities are available. A buyer should confirm which classifications can be reviewed or corrected, how corrections affect future behavior, and which actions require human approval.

### What happens if the agents disagree?

The system should follow the configured confidence, fallback, and escalation behavior rather than inventing certainty. A low-confidence or conflicting result should produce an approved fallback, a request for more information, or a human handoff. The exact fallback should be confirmed during setup.

### Does LobbyTeam invent answers?

It should not. LobbyTeam should use approved business knowledge and configured guidance. When it does not have enough information or confidence, it should say so appropriately, provide the approved fallback, collect context, or route to a human.

### Is the agent team secret sauce?

No. The existence of a coordinated team is useful buyer-facing differentiation. The implementation details, prompts, model choices, internal routing logic, and evaluation methods can remain private. Explain the operating model publicly, but do not expose security-sensitive implementation details.

## Who LobbyTeam Helps

### Is LobbyTeam useful for a solopreneur?

Yes. A solopreneur often loses work because one person must answer calls, schedule appointments, sell, support customers, and follow up. LobbyTeam gives that person a reliable front door, handles defined first-contact work, and makes the next action visible without pretending to replace the owner.

### How does LobbyTeam help a small office?

It can help the office answer recurring questions, handle configured appointment work, collect information, support visitors, and route requests to the right staff member. The office team receives context instead of a vague message and can focus its attention where human involvement matters most.

### How does LobbyTeam help an office manager?

It gives the office manager a way to define business rules, staff roles, schedules, routing paths, approved answers, and escalation rules. It can then apply those rules consistently across calls, messages, visitors, appointments, and follow-up workflows.

### How does LobbyTeam help a receptionist?

LobbyTeam can reduce repetitive interruptions, help with recurring questions and configured appointment tasks, and give the receptionist better context when taking over. It is intended to make the receptionist more effective, not make the receptionist disappear.

### How does LobbyTeam help an appointment-driven business?

It can support appointment questions, confirmations, reminders, rescheduling, cancellation workflows, intake, waitlists, and follow-up when the required calendar, permissions, and business rules are configured. Requests that need judgment or exception handling can be routed to staff.

### How does LobbyTeam help an online business?

It can provide a consistent first response across configured website, email, phone, SMS, or WhatsApp channels. It can answer approved questions, collect missing information, distinguish sales from support, start a workflow, and route the request with context instead of leaving it in an unowned inbox.

### How does LobbyTeam help field-service businesses?

It can collect service details while technicians are in the field, identify location or urgency, check service-area rules, route to a dispatcher or technician, and preserve the customer’s description. The goal is to turn a missed call into a useful intake and clearly owned callback.

### How does LobbyTeam help support leaders?

It can support after-hours and overflow coverage, route by region, schedule, expertise, urgency, and ownership, and preserve context between shifts. The support leader should be able to see what was handled, what was escalated, and what remains owned by a human.

### How does LobbyTeam help sales teams?

It can respond while intent is fresh, collect qualifying information, distinguish sales from support requests, identify fit or urgency according to business rules, and route a qualified opportunity to the appropriate sales owner with context.

### How does LobbyTeam help a physical lobby?

Visitors can engage through a kiosk, tablet, or QR code. LobbyTeam can answer approved questions, explain where to go, notify or route to a host, start a voice interaction when configured, or request a human connection. It must not be presented as a security or emergency system.

### Can LobbyTeam support after-hours and follow-the-sun operations?

It can support configured after-hours, overflow, and regional routing. For example, a U.S. after-hours request may route to an available India-based support team, while a request during U.S. business hours routes to the U.S. team. The actual live-transfer, callback, or asynchronous behavior depends on configuration.

### Can LobbyTeam send SMS?

WhatsApp workflows may be supported when configured. The business must confirm account, template, consent, message, and operational requirements for the intended use.

LobbyTeam can support email workflows when connected and configured. It may answer approved questions, collect missing details, send approved resources, classify the request, or route the conversation to a human owner.

A physical-lobby experience can be made available through a kiosk, tablet, or QR code. The business must provide the device, signage, connectivity, and physical operating instructions. LobbyTeam can then provide the configured visitor-assistance experience.

### Does the customer have to start the conversation?

The experience may be visitor-initiated through a call, message, chat, kiosk, tablet, or QR code. Proactive outreach is a separate workflow that must be configured and permitted. The business should define when LobbyTeam may initiate contact and what consent applies.

## Workflow And Human Handoff

### What can LobbyTeam do during an interaction?

Depending on configuration, it can answer approved questions, collect information, send approved resources, schedule or reschedule appointments, create a request, classify a need, route to a human, request a callback, or initiate another approved workflow.

### What happens when LobbyTeam does not know an answer?

It should not guess. It should acknowledge the limitation appropriately, use the configured fallback, ask for the missing information, provide an approved resource, or route the request to the right human. The buyer should define the fallback behavior for high-risk and high-value questions.

### How does a customer reach a human?

The configured workflow may support a live transfer, phone call, video call, callback request, SMS or email escalation, team notification, ticket, or asynchronous follow-up. The customer should be told what will happen next rather than being left with an unexplained “someone will contact you.”

### How does LobbyTeam decide who should receive the handoff?

The business can define signals such as request type, expertise, location, language, urgency, customer ownership, business hours, time zone, shift, availability, and primary or backup contacts. LobbyTeam applies those rules to guide the handoff.

### What context does the human receive?

The handoff should include the customer’s request, relevant answers, stated problem, urgency, requested next step, resources already shared, and the reason for escalation. The objective is for the human to continue the interaction without making the customer repeat everything.

### What if the preferred human does not answer?

The business should configure a fallback path. It may route to a backup person or team, create a callback task, send a message, create a ticket, or record an owned follow-up. The exact behavior should be stated during setup.

### Can LobbyTeam notify a human at the booth, office, or lobby?

Where the relevant notification and routing capability is configured, LobbyTeam can notify the designated human or team and provide the customer context needed for the response. Buyers should confirm the notification channel, response expectations, and backup behavior.

### Can LobbyTeam start a video call?

A video connection may be supported when configured. The buyer must verify the provider, availability, consent, fallback behavior, and whether the experience is a live connection or a request for a human to join.

### Can the business define high-priority requests?

Yes, the business should define the signals that make a request high priority. These may include urgency, customer type, account value, service level, issue category, timing, buying authority, location, or requested next step. LobbyTeam should apply those criteria rather than inventing its own definition.

## Setup And Configuration

### How long does setup take?

Setup time depends on the number of channels, workflows, people, routing rules, integrations, and approved knowledge the business wants to configure. The honest promise is:

> Start with what you know. Launch with the minimum useful workflow, then add detail as the team learns.

Do not promise a fixed setup time until it has been tested across real customer accounts.

### What does the business provide during setup?

The business should provide:

- Company and service information
- Products and common questions
- Approved answers and resources
- Target customer and visitor personas
- Workflows LobbyTeam may complete
- Human-only decisions
- Staff, expertise, schedules, and availability
- Routing and escalation rules
- Consent language
- Integration details
- Desired outcomes and success metrics

### Can setup be interactive?

A guided setup can ask focused questions while the business adds emails, text, PDFs, URLs, product pages, calendars, resources, and staff information. This helps the customer avoid starting from a blank page.

### Can the business use a worksheet instead?

Yes. A structured answer-and-upload worksheet can let the team collect information internally, then provide the completed material for configuration and review.

### What should the minimum launch configuration include?

At minimum, the business should define:

- What LobbyTeam is allowed to answer
- One useful workflow
- One human owner
- One backup path
- A safe fallback when information is missing
- The channel and entry point
- The consent language when contact data is captured
- The success measure for the workflow

### Can workflows be improved after launch?

They should be. The business can add approved knowledge, questions, resources, personas, routing rules, contacts, and workflows over time. The initial launch should not require every future scenario to be solved before the first useful workflow goes live.

### Does LobbyTeam require a large implementation project?

Not necessarily. A solopreneur may start with one channel and one workflow. A multi-location support operation may require more detailed routing, permissions, integrations, schedules, and testing. The implementation should match the operating complexity.

## Integrations And Operating Systems

### Can LobbyTeam work with an existing CRM?

It can work with an existing CRM when the integration is supported and configured. Buyers should confirm the supported method, fields, consent behavior, duplicate handling, retry behavior, and what happens when synchronization fails.

### Can LobbyTeam work with an existing calendar?

Calendar workflows may be supported when the calendar and permissions are connected. Buyers should confirm whether LobbyTeam can read availability, create appointments, reschedule, cancel, manage conflicts, and apply business-specific scheduling rules.

### Can LobbyTeam connect to ticketing or service-management tools?

It may connect through supported integrations, webhooks, or APIs. The buyer should verify the exact system, payload, authentication, fields, retry behavior, and whether LobbyTeam creates, updates, or only recommends a ticket.

### What happens when an integration fails?

The conversation and captured context should not disappear. The configured fallback should preserve the request, notify the appropriate owner, retry where supported, and make the failure visible for correction. Buyers should ask how long data is retained and how duplicates are prevented.

### Does LobbyTeam create another inbox?

It should reduce fragmented inbox work, not create an opaque new queue. A buyer should confirm where active requests, escalations, summaries, owners, and next actions are visible and how the team is notified.

### Can LobbyTeam route to multiple locations or teams?

Yes, when the routing rules and destinations are configured. Routing may use location, service area, department, account ownership, language, schedule, expertise, urgency, or regional coverage.

## Data, Consent, Safety, And Boundaries

### Who owns the customer data?

The customer’s business data should remain owned by the customer, subject to the service agreement and applicable law. The buyer should confirm export, retention, deletion, backup, subprocessors, and account-termination terms before deployment.

### Does LobbyTeam sell customer data?

The public privacy posture should be that customer data is not sold to third parties. The buyer should still review the privacy policy, service terms, subprocessors, analytics, communication providers, and applicable data-processing obligations.

### Is consent required?

Consent may be required before capturing contact information, recording communications, sending follow-up, or using certain messaging channels. The business is responsible for approving appropriate consent language and operating lawfully in the jurisdictions where it does business.

### Can LobbyTeam give medical, legal, financial, or other professional advice?

It should not be positioned as a substitute for licensed or accountable professional judgment. It can provide approved general information and route a request to the appropriate human when configured. Sensitive workflows require domain-specific review and guardrails.

### Can LobbyTeam make security or access decisions?

No. A physical-lobby experience can help with orientation and routing, but security personnel, access systems, and building management remain responsible for identity, access, incidents, restricted areas, and emergencies.

### Can LobbyTeam handle emergencies?

It should not be presented as an emergency response system. Emergency instructions and escalation should be defined separately by the business or building operator.

### Can LobbyTeam make promises to customers?

Only approved claims and actions should be available within the configured workflow. LobbyTeam should not invent product claims, pricing, refunds, delivery commitments, legal positions, or service guarantees.

### What happens if a customer asks for something outside the rules?

LobbyTeam should provide an approved fallback, collect context, or route to a human. It should not improvise a promise or conceal that human help is required.

## Measuring Value

### How should a buyer measure success?

Measure movement toward the desired outcome, not just conversation volume. Useful measures include:

- Missed calls reduced
- First-response time improved
- Requests resolved or completed
- Appointments booked, confirmed, or recovered
- Human handoffs with complete context
- Requests routed to the correct owner
- Follow-up completion
- After-hours requests covered
- Staff interruption reduced
- Customer repetition reduced
- Qualified sales conversations increased

### Does more interaction volume mean better performance?

No. A high number of conversations can hide poor qualification, incomplete requests, incorrect routing, or unnecessary escalation. The important question is whether more customers reach the right next step.

### How should human handoff quality be measured?

Evaluate whether the correct person received the request, the context was complete, the urgency was accurate, the customer avoided repetition, and the next action was completed within the expected time.

### How should a solopreneur measure value?

Start with one costly interruption or missed opportunity. Measure the number of requests that received a useful answer, were scheduled, were routed, or were followed up without the owner having to manually manage every first interaction.

## Pricing And Interaction Units

### How is LobbyTeam priced?

The working pricing concept has three monthly plans:

- Starter: $49/month for 20 Interaction Units, with $0.99 per additional unit
- Essential: $99/month for 75 Interaction Units, with $0.79 per additional unit
- Full Front Door: $299/month for 500 Interaction Units, with $0.59 per additional unit

Production pricing should be verified before publication.

### What is an Interaction Unit?

An Interaction Unit is one handled customer interaction, whatever the channel. It counts when LobbyTeam reaches a useful outcome such as an answer, completed message, scheduled request, or owned human next step. The billing guardrails are one connected phone minute, one email up to 300 words, one text message within the published size limit, one WhatsApp message up to 500 characters, or one website chat conversation up to 15 turns; turn 16 starts a new unit. Phone time rounds up, so 61 seconds is 2 units. Failed connections and misdials do not count.

### Are human handoffs charged as an additional unit?

The working model counts the communication that caused the handoff. Routing, summarization, and recording context should not create a surprise second charge by themselves. The final meter must be verified against production billing behavior.

### What happens when the included units are used?

The working posture is to notify the account owner at defined thresholds and continue under the published overage rate unless the account has chosen a hard limit. The final behavior should be clearly documented before launch.

### Why not price by seat?

LobbyTeam is intended to extend the front door across a business, not charge for every human who may receive a handoff. Interaction Units make usage easier to understand across phone, email, SMS, WhatsApp, and chat.

## Comparison Questions

### How is LobbyTeam different from an AI receptionist?

An AI receptionist usually focuses on answering a call or message. LobbyTeam is broader: a coordinated digital team engages the customer, interprets the request, applies business rules, completes configured work, routes to the right human or team, and preserves context for follow-through.

### How is LobbyTeam different from a chatbot?

A chatbot primarily provides a conversational interface. LobbyTeam is an operating layer for first contact. It can support multiple channels and move beyond answers into configured actions, routing, ownership, escalation, and follow-up.

### How is LobbyTeam different from a call center?

A call center provides human staffing and operational coverage. LobbyTeam is digital support that works alongside the business’s people and can provide the first response, handle defined workflows, and route exceptions. It does not automatically provide a human staffing team.

### How is LobbyTeam different from a virtual receptionist service?

A virtual receptionist service generally provides human or outsourced call handling. LobbyTeam provides a coordinated digital team that applies configured business guidance and routing rules, while connecting to the business’s own people or support operation when human ownership is needed.

### How is LobbyTeam different from voicemail?

Voicemail records a call. LobbyTeam aims to understand the request, answer what it can, gather context, identify urgency, route the request, and make follow-up visible.

### How is LobbyTeam different from call forwarding?

Call forwarding sends the interaction to a destination. LobbyTeam can apply rules about who should receive it, whether it can be handled first, which region or shift owns it, and what context the human should receive.

### How is LobbyTeam different from a lead form?

A lead form collects fields. LobbyTeam can engage in a conversation, ask follow-up questions, understand the stated need, provide approved information, qualify according to business rules, and guide the next action.

### How is LobbyTeam different from a lead scanner?

A lead scanner records that someone interacted or provided contact details. LobbyTeam is intended to create useful progress: answer the question, identify the need, route the request, and preserve context for the next conversation.

### How is LobbyTeam different from workflow automation?

Workflow automation usually executes predefined steps. LobbyTeam adds a customer-facing front door that can understand a request, select among configured paths, communicate with the customer, and route exceptions to humans.

## Buyer Evaluation

### What should I ask before buying LobbyTeam?

Ask:

- Which channels are live for my use case?
- Which workflows can LobbyTeam complete today?
- Which workflows only create a human follow-up?
- How does a human receive an escalation?
- What happens if the preferred human does not answer?
- Can I inspect and correct classifications?
- What data is retained and exportable?
- How are consent and contact capture handled?
- How does CRM or calendar synchronization work?
- How are duplicates and failures handled?
- How are Interaction Units counted?
- What is the actual success metric for my first workflow?

### What should I test in a proof of concept?

Test real examples:

- A routine question with an approved answer
- A request that requires missing information
- A scheduling or rescheduling request
- A high-priority or urgent request
- A request outside the approved knowledge
- A request requiring a specific specialist
- An after-hours request
- A failed or unavailable human handoff
- A cross-channel continuation
- A CRM or calendar failure

### What would make LobbyTeam a poor fit?

LobbyTeam may be a poor fit if the business:

- Does not want to define or maintain business guidance
- Needs a fully human-staffed call center rather than digital support
- Needs security, emergency response, or access-control decisions
- Cannot provide an accountable human owner for escalations
- Expects unrestricted autonomous decision-making
- Has no reliable channel, calendar, CRM, or workflow destination for the intended use case
- Wants to measure only total interaction volume rather than useful outcomes

### How should we start?

Start with one painful, measurable workflow. Examples:

- Missed appointment calls
- After-hours support requests
- Repetitive front-desk questions
- Website inquiries that receive no timely response
- Field-service calls that arrive while technicians are working
- Inbound leads that need qualification
- Physical visitors who arrive while the desk is occupied

Define the desired outcome, human owner, fallback, and success measure before expanding.
