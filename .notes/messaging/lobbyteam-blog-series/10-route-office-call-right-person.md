<p><strong>An office routes callers well when it uses the request, expertise, location, urgency, availability, and existing ownership to choose the next person. A blind transfer only moves the caller. A useful handoff carries the reason for the call and gives the next person enough context to continue without starting over.</strong></p>

<p>“Press 1 for sales, press 2 for support” can be useful when the categories are obvious. It breaks down when a customer does not know which department owns the answer, or when the first transfer reaches someone who is unavailable.</p>

<h2>Ask one question that improves the route</h2>

<p>The first question does not need to be an interrogation. It can be as simple as:</p>

<blockquote><p>What are you trying to get done today?</p></blockquote>

<p>The answer often tells the office more than a department label. A customer may need technical help, a replacement, a billing explanation, or a scheduling change. Those requests may belong to different people even when they concern the same product.</p>

<h2>Use routing signals deliberately</h2>

<p>Define which signals matter for each request:</p>

<ul>
<li>Required expertise.</li>
<li>Customer or account ownership.</li>
<li>Location or service area.</li>
<li>Urgency and timing.</li>
<li>Current availability and working hours.</li>
<li>Preferred contact channel.</li>
</ul>

<p>Routing should be explainable. If a customer is sent to a regional support team after hours, the business should know that the time zone and support schedule caused the route. If a technical question goes to a specialist, the original question should be visible.</p>

<h2>Design the unavailable-person path</h2>

<p>The right person may be on another call, out of the office, or not working that shift. Define the backup order before the busy moment. The fallback could be another specialist, a callback request, an email handoff, or a scheduled next step.</p>

<p>Tell the customer which path was chosen. A clear asynchronous handoff is better than promising a live transfer nobody can complete.</p>
