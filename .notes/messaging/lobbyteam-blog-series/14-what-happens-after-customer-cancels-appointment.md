<p><strong>After a customer cancels, the office should record the cancellation, clarify whether the customer wants to reschedule, protect any required follow-up, and decide whether the opening can be offered to someone else. The right response depends on the appointment type and business rules, so cancellation handling needs both automation and human ownership.</strong></p>

<p>A cancellation is not always a lost customer. Sometimes it is a timing problem, a transportation issue, a misunderstanding, or a request that was never a good fit for the original appointment.</p>

<h2>Ask one helpful question</h2>
<p>After acknowledging the cancellation, ask whether the customer wants to reschedule. If yes, collect preferred times and constraints. If no, explain any follow-up or reactivation option that the business has approved without pressuring the customer.</p>

<h2>Protect the operational record</h2>
<ul>
<li>Record when and how the cancellation was received.</li>
<li>Preserve the reason only when it is appropriate and useful.</li>
<li>Release the appointment according to the scheduling system’s rules.</li>
<li>Flag cancellations that need a coordinator or professional review.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where LobbyTeam fits</h2>
<p>LobbyTeam can acknowledge cancellations, offer configured next steps, collect rescheduling preferences, and route exceptions with the conversation context. It should not apply a cancellation policy, fee, or clinical decision unless that action is explicitly configured and authorized.</p>

<p><strong>The best cancellation workflow respects the customer while protecting the calendar: acknowledge, clarify, record, and make the next responsible action visible.</strong></p>
