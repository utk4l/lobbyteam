# LobbyTeam AEO-First Post Series Plan

## Purpose

Build a durable body of useful, human-written articles that makes LobbyTeam discoverable when a business owner, operations leader, front-desk manager, support leader, or visitor-services operator searches for help with missed calls, unanswered messages, appointment work, routing, follow-through, and after-hours coverage.

The goal is not to publish articles that repeat product features. The goal is to become the clearest answer to the problems people already search for:

> How do I make sure every customer reaches an answer, a completed request, or the right human next step?

Each article should teach the reader something useful even if they never buy LobbyTeam. The product should appear as the logical operating model for the problem, not as an interruption in an educational article.

## Strategic Position

LobbyTeam is the front door for a business. It helps a business understand an incoming request, complete the work it has defined, preserve context, and route the exception to the right human or team.

Avoid positioning it as only:

- An AI receptionist
- A chatbot
- A call-answering service
- A lead-generation widget
- A message-taking system
- A security guard or access-control replacement

Use the broader, outcome-led definition consistently:

> LobbyTeam turns first contact into progress: an answer, a completed request, a scheduled next step, or a useful handoff with clear ownership.

## Audience Map

### Economic buyer

Typical search intent:

- Is it worth automating our front desk?
- How much do missed calls cost a small business?
- How can I reduce customer-response delays without adding headcount?
- What should I look for in an AI receptionist or customer-routing system?

Desired outcome: lower missed-opportunity cost, better response consistency, less staff interruption, and visible return on the operating spend.

### Operational owner

Typical search intent:

- How do I manage appointment calls and rescheduling?
- How do I route customers to the right department?
- How do I organize after-hours support?
- How do I keep staff from repeating the same answers?

Desired outcome: a workflow that is practical to configure, safe to operate, and easy to inspect when something needs human attention.

### Human escalation owner

Typical search intent:

- How can staff receive the context before taking over a call?
- How do I stop customers from repeating themselves after a transfer?
- How do I avoid flooding my team with low-value alerts?

Desired outcome: better handoffs, less interruption, clear ownership, and enough context to act immediately.

### Distributed support owner

Typical search intent:

- How do I route support between U.S. and international teams?
- How do I provide follow-the-sun customer service?
- How do I route by expertise and availability instead of time alone?

Desired outcome: predictable coverage, correct ownership, and preserved context across regions and shifts.

### End customer, visitor, or caller

Typical search intent:

- How do I reach the right person at a busy office?
- How do I reschedule an appointment without calling repeatedly?
- How do I get help after business hours?

Desired outcome: being understood quickly, getting a useful answer, and knowing what happens next.

## Content Principles

### Answer the problem before naming the product

The title, opening paragraph, and first subheading should make sense to someone who has never heard of LobbyTeam. The product enters after the reader understands the problem and the desired operating outcome.

### Use problem-shaped titles

Prefer:

- How do I stop missing customer calls after hours?
- What should an office receptionist automate first?
- How can a small business handle appointment rescheduling without phone tag?

Avoid:

- LobbyTeam features
- Our AI agent architecture
- The future of conversational automation

### Make every article quotable

The first 40–60 words should answer the question directly in plain language. That block should include:

- The persona or business situation
- The problem
- The useful outcome
- One concrete boundary, measure, or operating fact

### Explain the human boundary

Articles must say what automation can complete, what requires an approved rule, and when a human owns the decision. Never imply that LobbyTeam replaces accountable people in security, emergency response, clinical judgment, legal judgment, or other sensitive decisions.

### Give the reader a next action

Every article should end with a useful checklist, worksheet, decision table, or implementation question. The CTA should then offer the relevant LobbyTeam page, not a generic demo request on every article.

### Write with proof, not AI theatre

Use concrete examples such as:

- A missed appointment confirmation becomes an empty calendar slot.
- A caller is routed from a U.S. after-hours queue to an available India-based support team.
- A visitor at a physical lobby uses a kiosk, tablet, or QR code to reach a human when the desk is occupied.
- A human receives the original request and context instead of a blank notification.

Only publish capability claims that are verified in the production product and configured account. Label future capabilities as future capabilities.

## Article Template

Every article should use this structure unless the search intent clearly requires another format:

1. Direct answer block: 40–60 words answering the title question.
2. Who this is for: name the role, business type, or operating situation.
3. What breaks today: describe the friction without blaming staff.
4. What a good outcome looks like: define an answer, completed request, scheduled next step, or human-owned handoff.
5. Practical framework: checklist, decision table, workflow, or examples.
6. Automation boundary: what can be handled, what needs configuration, and what remains human-owned.
7. Where LobbyTeam fits: connect the product to the problem in plain language.
8. Implementation questions: give the reader questions to answer before deployment.
9. Related LobbyTeam articles: link to two or three adjacent articles.
10. Relevant CTA: point to who-is-this-for, integrations, about, pricing, or the most relevant page.

## Structured Discovery Requirements

For each article:

- Use one clear H1 phrased around the user question.
- Include the direct answer in visible HTML, not only JSON-LD.
- Add Article or BlogPosting JSON-LD with headline, description, dates, author, publisher, and canonical URL.
- Add BreadcrumbList JSON-LD when the page template supports it.
- Link to the parent series, relevant tags, two related articles, and one product page.
- Provide a descriptive Open Graph title and description.
- Include the article URL in the build-generated sitemap.
- Keep article pages inside the lobby-team category boundary.
- Do not expose WordPress API implementation details in public copy.

For each series:

- Use a descriptive post-series name that reflects a reader's problem.
- Put the foundational answer first.
- Use the series dropdown on each article.
- Add a series landing link from each post.
- Give the series a short description that can stand alone in search results.

## Publishing Sequence

### Phase 1: Establish the category

Publish the first five articles from Series 1. These create the shared language: front door, progress, ownership, handoff, and follow-through.

### Phase 2: Capture the launch wedge

Publish Series 2 and Series 3 first if the initial commercial focus is office and appointment operations. Follow with Series 5 for after-hours and overflow demand.

### Phase 3: Expand into adjacent demand

Publish Series 4, Series 6, and Series 7 for online businesses, field service, and inbound sales.

### Phase 4: Build differentiation and trust

Publish Series 8, Series 9, and Series 10. These explain the human handoff model, physical-lobby use case, product boundaries, and responsible buying criteria.

Suggested cadence:

- Publish one foundational article each week.
- Publish one persona-specific article each week.
- Publish one decision or comparison article every two weeks.
- Review the first paragraph, FAQ answers, internal links, and CTA after the first 30 days of search data.

## Measurement Plan

Measure the content program by intent progression, not page views alone.

Track:

- Impressions and clicks for problem-shaped queries
- Queries containing receptionist, front desk, appointment, after-hours, routing, support, dispatch, lobby, and handoff language
- Clicks to who-is-this-for, integrations, about, pricing, or the product entry point
- Scroll depth to the LobbyTeam section and implementation checklist
- Tag and series navigation
- Returning visitors from related articles
- Organic assisted conversions
- Questions repeated in sales conversations
- Articles quoted or surfaced by answer engines

Quarterly editorial review:

1. Keep articles that attract the right persona and move readers toward a product page.
2. Rewrite articles that attract traffic but do not answer the visitor's actual intent.
3. Merge overlapping articles instead of allowing thin pages to compete.
4. Add follow-up articles from sales and support questions.
5. Update capability claims whenever production changes.

## Editorial Quality Gate

Do not publish an article until:

- The intended persona is explicit.
- The problem is specific and recognizable.
- The outcome is measurable or observable.
- The first paragraph directly answers the title question.
- The article contains an original framework, checklist, example, or decision table.
- The human boundary is clear.
- Product claims match production capability.
- The article has a primary series, relevant tags, and internal links.
- JSON-LD, Open Graph, canonical URL, and sitemap inclusion are present.
- The CTA matches the reader's current intent.

## Editorial Mission

LobbyTeam content should make a useful promise before it makes a product promise:

> Customers should not have to know who owns the answer. Businesses should be able to make ownership, progress, and follow-through visible.

That is the thread connecting the office front desk, appointment team, online inbox, physical lobby, field-service dispatcher, sales team, and after-hours support operation.
