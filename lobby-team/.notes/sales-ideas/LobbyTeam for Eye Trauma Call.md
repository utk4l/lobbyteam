# How LobbyTeam Could Change the Front Door for Eye Trauma Call

## The Opportunity In One Sentence

Eye Trauma Call already provides the specialist coverage hospitals and trauma programs need. LobbyTeam could make the path into that coverage faster, clearer, and more consistently owned for every emergency department, trauma program, transfer center, and referring clinician that reaches out.

This is not about putting an AI layer in front of clinical judgment. It is about making sure a request for ophthalmology support does not become an unanswered phone, an incomplete message, a repeated explanation, or an avoidable transfer.

## What Eye Trauma Call Says It Does Today

The public Eye Trauma Call site presents a tele-ophthalmology service for trauma programs and emergency departments. Its offer includes:

- Continuous 24/7/365 coverage
- Partial coverage to supplement local coverage
- Contingency coverage for unexpected, last-minute gaps
- Expedited ophthalmology evaluations
- Fewer unnecessary transfers
- A physician-designed clinical protocol
- Coverage intended to support hospitals, EMS, and patients

Source: [Eye Trauma Call](https://www.eyetraumacall.com/)

## The Front-Door Problem

The value of Eye Trauma Call is greatest when the right hospital reaches the right specialist at the right moment. The operational risk is everything that can happen before that connection is made.

An emergency department may:

- Call while the appropriate coordinator is already handling another request.
- Reach out after hours and be uncertain which number or process to use.
- Send a web inquiry without knowing how quickly someone will respond.
- Have a clinician who needs an immediate path to the on-call ophthalmology resource.
- Need to explain the same situation to multiple people.
- Be unsure whether to request a consultation, ask about coverage, or discuss a transfer.
- Need partial or contingency coverage quickly but not know who owns the request.
- Start with an incomplete message that forces a callback before anyone can act.

The problem is not necessarily a lack of intent. A hospital contacting Eye Trauma Call is likely already trying to solve an urgent operational or clinical access problem. The problem is making the next step obvious, safe, and owned.

## The LobbyTeam Position

> **LobbyTeam gives Eye Trauma Call a reliable digital front door for hospitals and trauma teams: it understands why they reached out, gets them to the right next step, and brings in the right human without making them start over.**

LobbyTeam could support the phone, website, email, and message-based entry points around the service while leaving clinical decisions and accountable medical judgment with qualified humans.

The outcome is:

> **A referring team reaches the right Eye Trauma Call pathway with less friction, better context, and clearer ownership.**

## What The Experience Could Feel Like

### Scenario 1: An emergency department needs coverage now

1. A clinician or transfer-center operator calls the published Eye Trauma Call number.
2. LobbyTeam identifies that the caller is seeking urgent ophthalmology support or coverage.
3. It collects only the operational information required by the configured workflow.
4. It follows Eye Trauma Call's approved escalation path.
5. It connects the caller to the configured human resource, requests an immediate callback, or routes the interaction to the appropriate coverage team.
6. The human receives the conversation context and the reason for escalation.
7. The interaction is logged so the team can see when the request arrived, what action was taken, and whether ownership was established.

The point is not for LobbyTeam to decide the medical answer. The point is to reduce the chance that a time-sensitive request sits in an unowned queue.

### Scenario 2: A hospital is evaluating partial or contingency coverage

1. An operations leader visits the website after a local coverage gap.
2. LobbyTeam answers questions about the available coverage options using approved Eye Trauma Call content.
3. It learns whether the visitor is exploring continuous, partial, or contingency coverage.
4. It collects the facility, role, contact details, geography, timing, and requested next step.
5. It sends an approved overview or coverage resource.
6. It routes the qualified inquiry to the appropriate Eye Trauma Call business or clinical contact.
7. The follow-up owner receives a concise summary instead of an empty contact-form notification.

The outcome is a better-qualified conversation while the hospital's interest is still active.

### Scenario 3: A referring clinician has a question outside the approved answer set

1. The clinician asks a question that requires medical, contractual, or operational judgment.
2. LobbyTeam does not guess or improvise.
3. It explains that a qualified Eye Trauma Call team member needs to respond.
4. It gathers the minimum context needed for the handoff.
5. It routes to the configured human or requests a callback through the approved channel.
6. The human receives the question and the conversation history.

The value is not pretending every question can be automated. The value is recognizing when human ownership is required and making that handoff useful.

### Scenario 4: A website visitor needs an immediate path, not a brochure

The website could offer clear entry choices such as:

- I need ophthalmology coverage for a trauma program.
- I need help with an unexpected coverage gap.
- I am an emergency department seeking a consultation path.
- I want to discuss how Eye Trauma Call works.
- I am an existing partner and need operational support.

LobbyTeam can then guide the visitor to the appropriate resource or human next step. The experience should never imply that a general website interaction is a substitute for emergency services or physician judgment.

## Where The Coordinated Team Matters

For Eye Trauma Call, the team story becomes concrete when it is tied to work the organization already has to do:

| LobbyTeam role | Eye Trauma Call value |
| --- | --- |
| Engaging agent | Speaks with the emergency department, hospital operator, trauma leader, or prospective partner. |
| Intent and context agent | Distinguishes coverage inquiry, urgent support request, existing-partner question, and general information request. |
| Knowledge agent | Uses approved Eye Trauma Call coverage descriptions, process guidance, and resources. |
| Workflow agent | Determines whether the request needs information, intake, callback, escalation, or a human connection. |
| Routing agent | Applies approved rules for the appropriate coordinator, clinical contact, business contact, region, schedule, or backup path. |
| Handoff agent | Gives the human the request, context, timing, and requested next step. |
| Record agent | Preserves the interaction, action taken, owner, and follow-up status. |

This is the meaningful explanation of “team.” It is not a claim that several autonomous systems are practicing medicine. It is a coordinated front-door workflow that helps the right people respond with context.

## The Business Outcomes To Sell

The strongest sales conversation should focus on operational outcomes, not AI features.

### Faster access to the right next step

Make it easier for a hospital or trauma team to reach the correct Eye Trauma Call resource without navigating an unclear process.

### Fewer abandoned or repeated requests

Reduce the number of inquiries that disappear in voicemail, forms, shared inboxes, or handoffs without ownership.

### Better context at the point of escalation

Give the responding human the original request, key details, timing, and requested outcome so the caller does not have to start over.

### Better coverage of high-intent inquiries

Separate an urgent support request from a general coverage question, then route both appropriately instead of treating every contact the same way.

### Stronger follow-through

Make callbacks, coverage discussions, partner questions, and open requests visible until someone owns the next step.

### More consistent service across channels

Give a phone caller, website visitor, and email sender a coherent path into the same operational process.

### Better evidence of service responsiveness

Create an interaction history that can help Eye Trauma Call understand inquiry volume, response paths, escalation patterns, and unresolved handoffs.

These are outcomes to validate with Eye Trauma Call. They should not be presented as measured results until baseline and post-launch data exist.

## What LobbyTeam Should Not Do In This Use Case

The healthcare context makes authority boundaries essential. LobbyTeam should not:

- Diagnose an eye injury.
- Determine clinical severity or treatment.
- Recommend whether a patient should be transferred.
- Replace an ophthalmologist, emergency physician, nurse, or qualified clinical coordinator.
- Promise clinical availability beyond the coverage schedule and configuration.
- Invent protocol, accreditation, response-time, pricing, or coverage claims.
- Collect more patient information than the approved workflow requires.
- Treat a general web inquiry as an emergency response channel.
- Make a caller wait for automation when the configured process requires immediate human escalation.

Recommended public boundary:

> **LobbyTeam helps people reach the right Eye Trauma Call pathway. It does not provide medical advice, diagnose patients, or replace the qualified professionals responsible for clinical decisions.**

The actual production workflow should be reviewed by Eye Trauma Call's clinical, legal, privacy, and operational owners before launch.

9. What should a hospital see or receive immediately after making contact?
10. How would Eye Trauma Call measure a better front door: response time, completed handoffs, fewer repeats, qualified coverage inquiries, fewer abandoned requests, or something else?

## A Possible Pilot

Start with a narrow, low-risk workflow rather than attempting to automate the entire clinical intake process.

### Pilot scope

- Website coverage inquiries
- General questions about continuous, partial, and contingency coverage
- Partner or hospital contact capture
- After-hours callback requests
- Human routing for existing partner support
- Interaction logging and ownership tracking

### Keep out of the first pilot

- Clinical diagnosis or triage
- Treatment recommendations
- Autonomous transfer recommendations
- Unreviewed patient-data collection
- Any workflow where the escalation owner and response expectation are undefined

### Pilot success measures

Agree on a baseline before launch. Candidate measures include:

- Percentage of inquiries receiving an immediate approved response
- Percentage of qualified inquiries with a named owner
- Time from inquiry to human acknowledgment
- Percentage of escalations containing usable context
- Number of repeated contacts for the same request
- Number of unowned or unresolved inquiries after the defined window
- Coverage inquiries separated from support requests
- Feedback from hospital and Eye Trauma Call staff

Do not promise that LobbyTeam will reduce transfers or improve clinical outcomes by itself. Those outcomes depend on the service, clinical team, hospital workflow, and many factors outside LobbyTeam. Position LobbyTeam as the front-door and coordination layer that can help Eye Trauma Call deliver its existing value more consistently.
