# How LobbyTeam Could Make FabrixMed's Front Door a Competitive Advantage

## The Big Idea

FabrixMed is building infrastructure for distributed healthcare: connecting hospitals, clinics, provider networks, specialist access, screening, virtual care, and care coordination across geographies. Its public site already gives prospective buyers many reasons to lean in, but the first step is primarily a general contact request.

LobbyTeam can turn that first step into an intelligent front door for two very different audiences:

1. **A prospective buyer who sees a FabrixMed solution and thinks, “This could help us. I want to understand more.”**
2. **An existing customer who needs technical support and wants the right human now, not a form receipt.**

The opportunity is not to add an AI receptionist. The opportunity is to make FabrixMed easier to understand, easier to buy from, and easier to get help from.

## What FabrixMed Already Offers

FabrixMed presents itself as “the digital fabric for borderless healthcare operations.” Its public site describes solutions including:

- **ConsultBridge:** provider-to-specialist consult workflows across care settings
- **CareScreen:** population-health and preventive-screening automation
- **ClinixBox:** virtual-first care and access to licensed clinicians and specialists
- **LuxeMed:** a premium virtual urgent-care concept marked as coming soon

FabrixMed also speaks to a wide range of audiences, including health plans, health systems, hospital systems, community health centers, emergency departments, long-term care, ACOs, CINs, microhospitals, hospice and palliative care, mobile health units, FQHCs, home health, correctional facilities, and direct patient care.

The public site emphasizes removing operational friction, expanding specialist access, improving coordination, and keeping clinical judgment with licensed healthcare professionals. LobbyTeam should reinforce that position rather than blur it.

## The Two Front-Door Problems

### Problem A: High-intent visitors have nowhere to go beyond “Contact us”

A visitor may arrive because they recognize a real problem:

- Their ED cannot reliably reach specialists.
- Their health system has transfer pressure or referral leakage.
- Their CHC has screening or diagnostic follow-up gaps.
- Their ACO needs better care-gap closure.
- Their long-term-care organization wants to reduce avoidable transfers.
- Their mobile-health program needs to close the loop after screening.
- Their organization needs a virtual-care model that can scale.

They may think:

> “This sounds relevant to us. I want to know which FabrixMed solution fits, what the workflow would look like, and whether someone understands our situation.”

A generic contact form captures intent but does not develop it. It does not help the visitor connect FabrixMed's capabilities to their own operating reality.

### Problem B: Existing customers need fast, intelligent technical support

An existing customer may call because:

- A workflow is not behaving as expected.
- A user cannot access a system or feature.
- A device, integration, or connection needs troubleshooting.
- A consult or screening workflow needs attention.
- A customer needs to know whether an issue is local, account-specific, or platform-wide.
- The U.S. support team is asleep, unavailable, or already handling another issue.

The customer does not want to submit another generic form. They want to reach the right technical support person, with enough context that they do not have to explain the problem repeatedly.

## The LobbyTeam Position For FabrixMed

> **LobbyTeam can become FabrixMed's always-available front door: helping prospective buyers understand where FabrixMed fits, while routing existing customers to the right support human based on the request, urgency, expertise, availability, and time of day.**

The value is not that LobbyTeam knows everything. The value is that it can understand the arrival, determine the appropriate path, and keep the next step owned.

Short version:

> **Turn “Contact us” into a useful conversation. Turn “I need help” into the right support path.**

## Audience One: The Visitor Who Thinks “This Could Help Us”

### The visitor's mental state

This person is often not ready to request a formal demo. They may be exploring, comparing options, or trying to translate a broad FabrixMed message into their own problem.

They want answers to questions such as:

- Which FabrixMed solution applies to an organization like ours?
- Is this for health systems, EDs, CHCs, ACOs, or our type of operation?
- Does this help with specialist access, screenings, transfers, virtual care, or care coordination?
- What does the workflow look like for our staff?
- Does it integrate with the systems we already use?
- Can this work across multiple sites or geographies?
- What happens after we submit an inquiry?
- Can I speak with someone who understands our operating problem?

### The LobbyTeam experience

Instead of a blank contact form, the site can invite the visitor to begin with their situation:

- **Tell us what you are trying to improve.**
- **Find the FabrixMed solution most relevant to your organization.**
- **See how specialist access, screening, virtual care, or coordination could fit your workflow.**
- **Ask a question and get the right person involved.**

LobbyTeam can then ask a small number of useful questions, such as:

- What type of organization are you with?
- Which care setting or workflow are you trying to improve?
- What is happening today that is not working well enough?
- Is the priority specialist access, screening, transfer reduction, virtual care, care coordination, or another problem?
- How many sites, teams, or locations are involved?
- Are you evaluating, piloting, replacing, or expanding a current process?
- What would a successful outcome look like?
- What would you like to do next: receive information, see a workflow, speak with a solutions specialist, or schedule a conversation?

The goal is not to force the visitor through a long qualification form. The goal is to help them articulate the problem and give FabrixMed a more valuable first conversation.

## Audience Two: The Existing Customer Who Needs Technical Support

### The customer experience FabrixMed should aim for

The existing customer calls or starts a support conversation and says:

> “I need help. I do not know which team handles this, and I cannot wait until someone notices my message.”

LobbyTeam should provide a fast first response, understand the issue category, determine urgency, and route to the right human based on:

- Customer or account
- Product or service involved
- Issue category
- Environment or deployment
- Severity and business impact
- Current time in the relevant region
- U.S. or India support schedule
- Human availability
- Required technical expertise
- Primary and backup escalation paths

### Example: U.S. daytime support

1. A customer calls or starts a support chat during U.S. business hours.
2. LobbyTeam verifies the customer and identifies the product or workflow involved.
3. It asks focused troubleshooting questions without pretending to diagnose beyond the approved support playbook.
4. It routes to the appropriate U.S.-based technical support person or queue.
5. The human receives the issue summary and conversation history.
6. The interaction remains logged with owner, severity, status, and next action.

### Example: U.S. nighttime support

1. The same customer reaches out outside U.S. support hours.
2. LobbyTeam evaluates the configured support schedule and current availability.
3. If the India support team is awake and assigned to that product or issue type, LobbyTeam routes the request there.
4. The India support human receives the context and handles the request according to FabrixMed's support policy.
5. If the issue requires a U.S.-specific owner or the India team is unavailable, LobbyTeam follows the backup rule: live escalation, callback request, ticket creation, or prioritized follow-up.
6. The customer is told exactly what will happen next.

### What makes this more than timezone forwarding

Simple timezone routing says:

> “It is daytime in India, so send the call to India.”

LobbyTeam should do more:

- Understand what the customer needs before routing.
- Route by expertise, not only by clock.
- Avoid sending a billing issue to a technical specialist.
- Identify whether the issue is urgent, blocked, degraded, or informational.
- Send the human the context needed to act.
- Use availability and backup rules, not assumptions.
- Preserve the customer's place in the workflow if a live human cannot respond.

The promise is:

> **Route the customer to the right awake human, not merely the nearest open queue.**

## The Support Routing State Machine

FabrixMed's customer-support deployment should be designed as an explicit workflow rather than a vague “route to India after hours” rule.

### States

1. **New support arrival**: call, chat, email, SMS, or other configured channel is received.
2. **Customer identified**: account and contact are verified according to FabrixMed's approved process.
3. **Issue understood**: product, workflow, symptom, impact, and desired help are captured.
4. **Severity classified**: informational, degraded, blocked, urgent, or another FabrixMed-approved classification.
5. **Knowledge response available**: an approved answer or troubleshooting step can be provided.
6. **Human required**: the issue needs technical judgment, access, investigation, or customer-specific action.
7. **Routing selected**: U.S. or India team selected using schedule, availability, expertise, and backup rules.
8. **Human handoff pending**: live transfer, callback, ticket, or team notification is in progress.
9. **Human accepted**: a named support owner has taken responsibility.
10. **Follow-up pending**: a human cannot respond immediately, but the request has an owner and next action.
11. **Resolved or closed**: the issue is resolved, referred, or closed according to FabrixMed's support policy.

### Routing rule example

```text
If customer support request:
  identify account, product, issue type, severity, and required expertise
  if approved immediate escalation condition:
    route to the available qualified human using the current schedule
  else if U.S. support is open and assigned specialist is available:
    route to U.S. support
  else if India support is open and assigned specialist is available:
    route to India support
  else:
    create the configured follow-up path with owner, priority, and response expectation
```

This is a concept for design review, not a production clinical or support policy. FabrixMed must define the actual schedules, contacts, severity levels, fallback rules, and response expectations.

## What LobbyTeam Must Not Do

FabrixMed operates in healthcare, so the guardrails must be explicit.

LobbyTeam should not:

- Provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment recommendations.
- Decide patient disposition, transfer necessity, or clinical urgency.
- Replace licensed clinicians or FabrixMed's accountable support staff.
- Expose protected health information unnecessarily.
- Ask for patient details when an operational description is sufficient.
- Promise system availability, response time, accreditation, security, or compliance without verified source material.
- Route a customer solely by time zone when expertise or availability points elsewhere.
- Treat every support request as a sales lead.
- Tell customers that a human has accepted an issue before that has actually happened.
- Hide an unresolved request behind a generic acknowledgment.

Recommended external boundary:

> **LobbyTeam helps FabrixMed guide inquiries and support requests to the right next step. It does not replace licensed clinical judgment or the technical professionals responsible for customer support.**

## Suggested Pilot Sequence

### Phase 1: Website opportunity concierge

Start with prospective visitors because this has lower operational risk than technical support and can immediately improve the current contact-form experience.

Scope:

- Organization-type discovery
- Care-setting and problem discovery
- Product-path guidance
- Approved resource delivery
- Demo or conversation request
- Qualified human handoff
- Inquiry logging

### Phase 2: Existing-customer support intake

Add a clearly separated “Existing customer support” path.

Scope:

- Customer verification
- Product and issue identification
- Approved first-response guidance
- Severity and impact capture
- Support ticket or human escalation
- U.S./India routing based on schedule and expertise
- Support interaction logging

### Phase 3: Live routing and follow-the-sun operations

Once rules and ownership are tested:

- Add live phone routing where appropriate.
- Add callback workflows.
- Add team notifications and escalation timers.
- Add support-status and outage-aware behavior.
- Add reporting across regions and shifts.

### Phase 4: Continuous improvement

Use interaction data to improve:

- Website questions and prompts
- Product-to-problem mapping
- Support knowledge resources
- Routing rules
- Escalation thresholds
- Staffing and schedule coverage
- The questions FabrixMed's buyers and customers ask most often
