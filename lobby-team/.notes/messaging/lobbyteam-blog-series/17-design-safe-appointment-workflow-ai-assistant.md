<p><strong>A safe appointment workflow for an AI assistant starts with authority boundaries: define what the assistant may answer, what it may change, what requires confirmation, and who owns exceptions. The assistant should preserve context and guide the next step, while the business remains accountable for calendar access, policies, sensitive requests, and professional judgment.</strong></p>

<h2>Define the workflow states</h2>
<ul>
<li>Request received and identity or contact details collected as appropriate.</li>
<li>Appointment type and constraints understood.</li>
<li>Availability presented from an approved source.</li>
<li>Booking, confirmation, cancellation, or rescheduling completed.</li>
<li>Human review requested, accepted, completed, or still pending.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Define failure behavior before launch</h2>
<p>Decide what happens when the calendar is unavailable, the customer gives conflicting information, a required field is missing, the request is urgent, or the first human does not respond. A safe fallback captures the request and explains what happens next instead of pretending the action succeeded.</p>

<h2>Give the customer a correction path</h2>
<p>Customers should be able to say the appointment is wrong, the reminder is unclear, or they need a person. The system should preserve the original request and the correction, so the human does not begin with an empty ticket.</p>

<h2>Where LobbyTeam fits</h2>
<p>LobbyTeam provides a coordinated digital front-door team that can engage across configured channels, apply approved appointment rules, and route exceptions with context. It is not a substitute for the accountable people who own sensitive, professional, or judgment-heavy decisions.</p>

<p><strong>Safe appointment automation is not about giving an assistant unlimited authority. It is about making routine progress reliable and making the boundary to human ownership visible.</strong></p>
